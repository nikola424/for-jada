D1: The Costly Mistake Most Students Make With Scholarships

Preview Free: the 3 things I’d do instead

Why the big scholarships are the hardest to win

Most students apply for scholarships the same way. They search “scholarships”, find the big national ones, and apply to those.

And so does everyone else. Thousands of students, one check.

So here’s what I’d do instead, in 3 simple steps:

Start local

Go niche

Track every deadline

1: Start local

Your city, your church, your parents’ jobs, your school district. These get a handful of applicants, sometimes fewer.

2: Go niche

Scholarships for your major, your heritage, your HBCU, your hobby. The more specific the scholarship, the fewer people apply.

3: Track every deadline

Put every scholarship in one list with its due date. Most money is lost to deadlines nobody wrote down.

Start local. Go niche. Track every deadline.

D4: You DON’T Need A 4.0

Preview Why your GPA matters less than you think

The overlooked truth about GPA

When a student sees “3.5 GPA required”, they usually close the tab and give up on scholarships altogether.

That’s the wrong lesson.

Plenty of scholarships ask for a 2.5. Plenty don’t ask for a GPA at all. They want an essay, a story, a reason you deserve it.

The students I see win most aren’t the ones with the best grades. They’re the ones who apply the most, on time, with a story that’s clearly theirs.

This isn’t for the student looking for one big check to fix everything.

It’s for the student willing to stack smaller wins until college is paid for.

D5: Write The Essay That Wins [COPY ME]

Preview Free: the 3-part way I structure every scholarship essay

How to write one story you can use again and again

Part 1: The moment. Open with one specific moment from your life, in two or three sentences. Not “I have always been passionate about…”

Part 2: What it taught you. What changed in you because of that moment, and what you did about it.

Part 3: Where it’s taking you. Your goal, and how this scholarship helps you get there.

Write it once. Then change Part 3 a little for each scholarship.

Copy it, fill it in with your own story, and send one this week.

D6: 3 Ways To Get A Recommendation That Helps

Preview Free: who to ask, and when

The part most students leave too late

1. Ask a teacher who saw you grow, not the one who gave you the best grade.

2. Ask at least three weeks before the deadline, so they’re not writing it in a rush.

3. Send them a short note with your goal and two things you’re proud of, so they have something real to write about.

Do these three and your recommendation reads like it’s about you, not about a student.

D7: 3 Types Of Scholarships Nobody Applies To

Preview The ones I’d start with tomorrow

Where the money most students miss is hiding

I’ve kept the biggest one for last.

Most students spend all their time on scholarships that thousands of people apply to. Here are 3 types that get a fraction of that, and where I’d start tomorrow.

1. Local scholarships

Community groups, churches, local businesses and your school district. They’re rarely online in one place, which is exactly why so few students apply. Ask your counselor for the local list, and check your city’s community foundation.

2. Niche scholarships

For your major, your background, your HBCU, your hobby, even your parents’ employer. The more specific the scholarship, the smaller the pile it sits in.

3. Small scholarships

$500 and $1,000 scholarships feel like they’re not worth the time. But they get far fewer applicants, and five of them add up to a semester of books and fees.

Most of the $600K I won came from thinking this way: lots of applications, sent on time, to the ones other students skip.

If you want a plan for your child built around exactly this, that’s what I do one-on-one.
